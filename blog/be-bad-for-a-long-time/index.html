Be Bad at It for a Long Time

A post with 55,000 likes says the main reason people fail is simple: they won't be bad at something for a long time. The Bible has been saying the same thing for two thousand years, with better promises attached.

From the post by @theoffkid_, October 2026. Scripture quotes are KJV (public domain).

01The suck is the tuition

"The main reason why most people don't succeed at anything is because they're not prepared to be bad at something for a long time."

That's the whole post. Nineteen words, and it rewired a lot of brains this week. Because it's annoyingly true.

Everyone wants the skill. Nobody wants the season of being terrible at it. But the terrible phase is not a detour on the road to good. It is the road. Every guitarist you admire was once a person making sounds that should have been illegal. Every founder you admire once pitched something nobody wanted. The tuition for getting good is a long stretch of being bad, paid in full, in advance, with no refunds.

Here's the part nobody likes: you can't skip it, buy your way out of it, or morning-routine your way around it. You can only outlast it.

02God doesn't waste the suck

The Bible has a whole theology of the bad phase. It just calls it by older names: patience, endurance, longsuffering.

Paul lays out the actual manufacturing line in Romans: tribulation works patience, patience works experience, experience works hope. Read that again. Tribulation doesn't just vaguely lead to hope someday. It manufactures it, step by step, like an assembly line. The suck is not wasted time. It is raw material.

"And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; and patience, experience; and experience, hope."Romans 5:3-4

James says the same thing from the other end: the trying of your faith works patience, and if you let patience finish her work, you come out "perfect and entire, wanting nothing." The bad phase has a completion date. Most people just quit before it arrives.

"Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing."James 1:3-4

And Jesus told an entire parable for the quitters. The persistent widow in Luke 18 kept knocking on the judge's door until he caved from sheer exhaustion. Jesus told that story, Luke says, so that "men ought always to pray, and not to faint." Heaven, apparently, rewards the people who won't stop knocking.

"And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint."Luke 18:1

03Due season

The promise I come back to most is in Galatians: "let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."

Due season. The harvest has a schedule, and you don't control it. A farmer doesn't dig up his seeds every week to check on them. He waters, he waits, he trusts the season. Your job is the sowing and the staying. The timing belongs to God.

"And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."Galatians 6:9

Hebrews calls the whole thing a race: "let us run with patience the race that is set before us." Not a sprint. A race. Running with patience sounds like a contradiction until you realize it's the only combination that finishes.

"Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us."Hebrews 12:1

The verdict

  • Being bad is not a sign you're on the wrong road. It is the road.
  • The winners aren't the most talented. They're the ones still showing up at month eight, sounding terrible.
  • God doesn't skip the suck. He uses it: tribulation makes patience, patience makes experience, experience makes hope.
  • Don't dig up the seed. Due season is coming, if you faint not.

Most people quit at month five. Be the weirdo still practicing at month nine.
