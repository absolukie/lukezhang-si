The City Where the Lock Is Optional

A viral post claims Dubai is so safe that locking your door has only downsides. The numbers say the vibe is real.

From a post by Lucas (@lukecarry_), October 10, 2026. 1.2M views and counting.

Lucas @lukecarry_ · Oct 10, 2026

Dubai is probably the first city I've lived in where I only see downsides to locking my door when I'm not home

1.2M views · 9.2K likes · 185 reposts

Verdict

  • Mostly true. Dubai is one of the safest cities on earth by every available measure, and its burglary risk sits far below the point where locking up earns its keep.
  • The UAE is the safest country in the world for the second year running (Numbeo 2026, safety score 86.0). Dubai itself scores 83.8, sixth safest city worldwide.
  • The "only downsides" part is doing comedic work. There is still a nonzero burglary risk, and your insurer may disagree. But directionally, he is right.

01How safe is Dubai, really?

Dubai at 83.8 sits sixth in the world, behind four other Gulf cities and Taipei. For context, that is roughly ten points safer than Singapore and nearly ten points safer than Seoul.

02The specific fear: someone breaking in

The index also asks one very on-the-nose question: how worried are you about your home being broken into and things stolen? Lower is better. This is the number Lucas is feeling in his bones.

"Worries home broken and things stolen"

Dubai's 13.9 reads as "Very Low," from 1,160 contributors. Abu Dhabi is lower still at 9.2. Compare Shanghai at 23.8 or Seoul at 21.3: the worry in Dubai is roughly half.

03Why it is this safe

Three things stack in Dubai's favor. The laws are strict and the penalties for theft are severe, which keeps the expected cost of burglary high for the burglar. The city is dense with cameras and police presence, which keeps the chance of getting caught high. And most residents are expats whose visas depend on a clean record, which keeps the willing population small. Low reward, high risk, few takers.

04The lock math

Here is the part Lucas intuited. Locking your door is not free: it costs you seconds every day and the occasional lockout. Not locking risks a burglary. Somewhere there is a breakeven burglary probability where the lock earns its keep. Move the sliders and find it.

Nobody publishes Dubai's actual burglary rate, so treat this as a thought experiment, not a statistic. But with "very low" worry across more than a thousand locals, the real risk sits far below any reasonable breakeven line. That is the whole post in one number.

05The honest caveats

  • Numbeo is a perception survey, not police data. It measures how safe people feel, which is exactly what Lucas is describing, but it is not a burglary rate.
  • The UAE does not publish detailed crime statistics, so independent verification has limits.
  • "Only downsides" is hyperbole. A nonzero risk remains, and home insurance policies often require locked doors to pay out.
  • Safety this high has its own price: it is downstream of surveillance and strict laws that many people would not trade for.
