What Is a FLOP?

aiengineeringResearched October 2026

Every AI number you hear, training cost, chip speed, API prices, is the same unit wearing different clothes: the FLOP. One FLOP is one floating-point operation, a single piece of arithmetic. This primer covers what a FLOP is, why training is billed in hundreds of sextillions of them, why your phone does trillions per second without noticing, and why the whole industry is an argument about who gets to do the most of them. The sentence to remember: a FLOP counts the work done, FLOP/s measures how fast, and AI runs on both.

1. The brief

Open a GPU spec sheet and you will see TFLOPS. Read an AI paper and you will see training FLOPs. Listen to a lab CEO and you will hear about compute. All three are the same unit. A FLOP is one floating-point operation: one piece of arithmetic on numbers with decimal points, like multiplying 2.5 by 3.7. Neural networks are, underneath everything, enormous piles of exactly this kind of arithmetic, so the FLOP became the industry's unit of account.

Chip speed is measured in FLOP/s (operations per second). A training run's total bill is measured in FLOP (no slash). Confuse the two and every number in AI becomes nonsense; keep them straight and you can read a GPU spec, a training-cost headline, and a cloud price list like they are the same document, because they are. The sentence to remember: FLOP counts the work done, FLOP/s measures how fast it gets done.

The collision

Amodei and Altman say the path is more compute, bought in ever-larger piles, because intelligence is the thing you can scale with money. LeCun says the pile is aimed at the wrong target: scaling text prediction to human-level intelligence is "simply false" (his words to MIT Technology Review), and his $1.03B AMI Labs bet is that the future belongs to world models that learn how reality behaves instead of predicting the next word. Epoch AI sits in the middle with a spreadsheet: the trend is 5x per year whether anyone likes it or not, but the fastest-falling number in AI is not the training bill, it is the price of running last year's model.
