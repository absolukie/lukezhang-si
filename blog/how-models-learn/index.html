How Models Learn From Raw Text

llmsaiResearched October 2026

Every chatbot and every image generator was trained with almost no human labeling. The trick is self-supervision: the data is its own answer key. Text models learn by predicting the next token; image models learn by predicting noise. This primer covers both objectives, the papers that defined them, the voices behind them, and where the two paradigms are colliding.

1. The brief

Training a frontier model looks impossible on paper. Nobody labeled trillions of words with correct answers, and nobody drew boxes around billions of images. The field's central insight is that the labels were hiding in the data all along. Take any sentence, cover up a word, and the sentence itself is the answer key. Take any image, add noise, and the original pixels are the target. That is self-supervised learning: objectives where the supervision signal is manufactured from the raw data, for free, at any scale. Two families grew from it. Autoregressive models like GPT predict the next token, one step at a time. Diffusion models like the ones behind text-to-image generators predict the noise that was added to an image, one denoising step at a time. And in 2025, the two met: LLaDA trained a language model with a diffusion objective. The sentence to remember: models learn because the data grades its own homework.

The model only ever answers one question

Given the tokens so far, what comes next? A language model never writes a sentence in one go; it bets on one token, appends it, and bets again. Four sentences, one blank each. Tap the blank to see what a toy model guesses, then step through with the arrows. Every tap is one step.

The probabilities are illustrative, not measured from a real model. A real model outputs a full probability distribution over its entire vocabulary at every position, tens of thousands of tokens, and the probabilities always sum to one. The sharpness of the guess is the training working: more training makes confident contexts spikier and uncertain contexts flatter.

Diffusion generation, click by click. Start with pure static, the way every image begins. Each tap removes one slice of noise, the way the trained model does. Nothing is drawn; the picture was always under the noise. Every tap is one step.

100%noise remaining

Toy model, not a trained network: the noise here is a fixed random field blended out in equal slices, and the "picture" is a hard-coded smiley. A real diffusion model predicts a fresh noise field at every step, conditioned on the text prompt, and the denoising schedule is tuned, not even. The shape of the idea is exact; only the numbers are play-pretend.

4. Key voices

Five people who built the objectives this primer runs on. Each view is one or two sentences with the primary source linked. The real action is at the bottom, where the token predictors collide with the representation predictors.

The big disagreement

Same goal, opposite bets. One switch, every tap flips. No picking sides, just the collision.

The generative camp GPT, BERT, diffusion lineage
predict the raw stuff

The next token is enough. Radford et al. showed in 2018 that predicting what comes next on raw text transfers to a dozen tasks with barely any fine-tuning, and GPT-3 proved at 175B parameters that in-context learning emerges from the same objective with zero architecture change. The diffusion side bets the same way on pixels: predict the noise at every step, and images fall out. Language Models are Few-Shot Learners, May 2020.

Yann LeCun Turing laureate, AMI Labs founder
the dissenter

Predicting raw tokens is predicting the wrong thing. His cake: if intelligence is a cake, the bulk of it is self-supervised learning, with supervised learning as the icing and reinforcement learning as the cherry. But the self-supervision should predict abstract representations of the world, not the next word or the next pixel: generative models waste effort filling in details that are inherently unpredictable. That is the JEPA program, his "path towards autonomous machine intelligence." JEPA and world models, 2026.

The irony the middle ground points out: LeCun called the recipe right (pre-train self-supervised, then fine-tune, then RL) while calling the objective wrong. The industry's frontier models follow his cake and ignore his warning.

5. What comes next

Every prediction below is attributed: who said it, where, when. The badge is my read of how solid it is: consensus means multiple independent sources agree, contested means smart people disagree, speculative means one person's leap. Predictions go stale; this section is frozen as of October 2026.

Self-supervised pre-training stays the foundation of every frontier modelconsensus
GPT-1 (2018), BERT (2018), GPT-3 (2020), and LLaDA (2025) all pre-train on raw data with labels manufactured from the data itself. Nobody in the field is proposing a return to hand-labeled training at scale; the disagreement is only about which self-supervised objective.
The through-line across all five arXiv papers in the Sources section, 2018 to 2025.
LLaDA's authors expect diffusion to rival autoregressive models at frontier scalecontested
Their finding: diffusion models are "a viable and promising alternative" to autoregressive models, challenging the assumption that in-context learning and instruction following are inherently tied to next-token prediction. LLaDA 8B already matches LLaMA 3 8B on in-context learning.
Nie et al., "Large Language Diffusion Models," arXiv:2502.09992, Feb 2025.
LeCun expects predictive world models to displace generative token predictioncontested
His bet: models that predict abstract representations of how the world changes (JEPA) will win over models that generate raw tokens or pixels, because the latter waste capacity on unpredictable detail. The counter-bet from the generative camp is that scale keeps making the "wrong" objective work.
LeCun's "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" program, described in the JEPA piece linked below; the generative counter-position is the GPT-3 result.
One denoising objective could eventually train both text and image modelsspeculative
LLaDA put a diffusion objective on text; latent diffusion put one on images. If masked denoising keeps scaling on both sides, future multimodal models may train on a single unified objective: corrupt the data, predict what was removed, repeat. Nobody has built that model yet; it is the shape the field is drifting toward.
My read of the LLaDA plus latent-diffusion trajectory, Oct 2026; no lab has announced such a model.
