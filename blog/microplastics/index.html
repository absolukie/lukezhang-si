You're Part Plastic Now

The newest research stopped asking if microplastics are inside us and started measuring how much. The numbers are worse than the old estimates.

All findings below are from peer-reviewed papers, 2024–2026. Sources linked at the end.

01Your brain is 0.5% plastic

In 2025, researchers weighed the plastic in human brains. The median: about 4,917 micrograms per gram of brain tissue. That is roughly half a percent of your brain, by weight, made of plastic. Mostly polyethylene, the stuff in shopping bags.

It got there fast. Brains from people who died in 2024 had about 50% more plastic than brains from 2016. Eight years, half again as much. Brains from dementia patients carried 3 to 5 times more than healthy brains, though nobody has shown which direction that arrow points.

Your brain holds 7 to 30 times more plastic than your liver or kidneys. The blood-brain barrier, it turns out, is more of a suggestion.

The picture so far

  • Microplastics are in every organ anyone has bothered to check.
  • The amounts are rising fast, not flat.
  • Human studies show associations with disease, not proof of cause. The animal and cell studies are uglier.

03How it gets in

Three main doors, all recently re-measured upward.

Bottled water240,000 particles / liter
90% are nanoplastics under 1 micron. 10 to 100 times what older methods could even see. (Qian et al., PNAS 2024)
One plastic tea bag1.2 billion particles / mL
Polypropylene bags shed the most. First proof that these particles get taken up by human intestinal cells, into the cell nuclei. (Banaei et al., Chemosphere 2024)
Indoor air, per day68,000 particles inhaled
About 100 times earlier estimates, because new methods count the 1–10 micron particles everyone missed. Cars are worse than homes. (Yakovenko et al., PLOS ONE 2025)

The old "credit card a week" line (5 grams) is still quoted everywhere, but it was a rough 2019 extrapolation. The 2025 inhalation numbers alone suggest the real total is much higher. Nobody has published a solid new combined total yet.

04What it might be doing

This is where honesty matters. Almost every human study is observational: plastic is there, disease is there, causation is unproven. But the associations keep getting stronger, and the lab work fills in plausible mechanisms.

  • Heart: In 304 carotid-artery surgery patients, those with microplastics in their arterial plaque had 4.5 times the risk of heart attack, stroke, or death over the next three years. (Marfella et al., NEJM 2024)
  • Heart, again: Microplastics turned up in the coronary blood of 84% of heart-attack patients versus 32% of people with clean arteries. (Barbato et al., European Heart Journal 2026)
  • Blood clots: Found in 80% of clots from stroke and heart-attack patients; more plastic tracked with higher D-dimer, a clot marker. (Wang et al., eBioMedicine 2024)
  • Lungs: Polystyrene nanoparticles made healthy lung cells migratory, damaged their DNA, and impaired DNA repair. Early cancer indicators, in a dish. (Ernhofer et al., J. Hazardous Materials 2025)
  • Liver: In mice, chronic realistic doses clogged the liver's cleanup cells until they couldn't clear pathogens and fat built up. (Mass et al., Nature Metabolism 2026)
  • Fertility: Found in 100% of tested human testes (Yu et al., 2024), in 69% of follicular fluid and 55% of seminal fluid samples (ESHRE 2025), and ~20% higher in placentas from miscarriages (Wang et al., eBioMedicine 2025).

05The planet's dose

It is not just us. A 2025 Nature paper estimated 27 million tonnes of nanoplastics in the upper 200 meters of the North Atlantic alone. Ten times every prior estimate of all plastic in the ocean, of every size. The authors' own words: it cannot be cleaned up.

Microplastics turned up in all 227 European farm fields tested. About 219 tonnes settle out of Swiss air every year. 88% of protein foods sampled in the US had microplastics, and heavily processed food had more.

So what do you actually do

  • Water: filter your tap water instead of bottled. Biggest single lever in the data.
  • Heat: don't microwave in plastic, don't pour boiling water through plastic tea bags. Heat multiplies shedding.
  • Air: ventilate, vacuum with a HEPA filter, and know cars are the worst indoor air in the data.
  • Food: less heavily processed protein, less plastic packaging. The 2024 protein study found processing mattered more than land vs. sea.
  • None of this gets you to zero. The air study alone makes that clear. It gets you less.
