My AI Has Its Own Computer

Every AI agent I used before lived on my laptop. Muse lives on its own computer, and I talk to it from my phone. That one architectural difference changes everything about when and where work gets done.

Everything in this post was assigned, built, and shipped from my iPhone. Including this sentence.

01My whole setup fits in my pocket

I barely open my laptop anymore. Here is a normal day, all from my phone:

  • I sent a screenshot of my blog and wrote "update this with the new carousel." Twenty minutes later it was live.
  • I pasted a tweet and wrote "add this to my design skill." New skill, saved and searchable.
  • I pasted a YouTube link and wrote "make a blog post with a visualizer." An hour later: a published post with an interactive card deck.
  • I told it to keep retrying a restaurant booking in Beijing at 8pm tonight. It will make the calls while I sleep.

That is not a chat app. That is a computer I talk to.

02Where the work actually happens

Here is the difference, and it is the whole game.

Most AI agents run on your computer. You open a terminal, the agent lives in that terminal, and when you close the laptop the agent goes to sleep with it. Some of them have phone apps, but the moment real work starts, running code, testing, deploying, it needs your machine awake and nearby. Your phone is a second-class citizen: fine for chatting, useless for shipping.

Muse flipped it around. The agent runs on its own dedicated computer. It has a terminal, a browser, and a filesystem, and it keeps all of them between conversations. My phone is not a companion to that computer. It is a complete remote control for it.

So the work doesn't happen on my phone. It doesn't happen on my laptop either. It happens on Muse's computer, and the phone is enough to drive all of it.

03What that unlocks

Three things the other setup can't do

  • Fire and forget. I assign work, put the phone down, and get a link back. No watching a terminal scroll. No "don't close this tab."
  • It works while I sleep. Scheduled jobs run on its computer, not mine. The restaurant retry fires at 8pm. My laptop will be closed. It doesn't matter.
  • Dictation becomes the interface. I talk in short bursts from the couch; it does the long quiet work. The phone was always great at input and terrible at output. Now the output happens somewhere else.

04The pocket test

Pick a task, then tap through both setups step by step and watch what happens. The top lane is Muse. The bottom lane is a terminal-based agent, the kind that lives on your laptop.

05The verdict

The phone was never the limitation. The limitation was that every agent needed my computer to do the work, and my computer needed me sitting next to it. Give the agent its own computer and suddenly the phone is enough.

My laptop is now a very expensive Netflix machine. It has never been happier.
