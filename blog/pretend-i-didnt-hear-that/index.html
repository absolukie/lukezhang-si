I'm Going to Pretend I Didn't Hear That

My pastor preached on toxic self-talk this week. The fix is simpler than I expected: you don't have to attend every argument you're invited to, especially the ones in your own head at 2am.

From the sermon "How to fix Toxic Self-Talk" by Shaun Nepstad, Fellowship Church, October 2026. Scripture quotes are KJV (public domain).

01The accuser has one move

Revelation calls the devil "the accuser." That's his whole job description. He can't change what God has said about you, so he goes after the next best thing: your confidence in it.

His opening line never changes. In Genesis 3 it was "Did God really say?" and he's been running the same play ever since. Did God really forgive that? Did God really call you loved? Are you sure that promise is for you?

The target is always joy first. Nehemiah 8:10 says the joy of the Lord is your strength, so joy is what gets attacked first. A Christian with no joy has no strength, and a Christian with no strength is easy to push around.

"The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy."John 10:10

02You don't have to attend

Here's the line that stuck with me most: you don't have to attend every argument you're invited to.

Most of us treat every accusing thought like a summons we must answer. We show up, we argue back, we replay the case for hours. But 2 Timothy says to have nothing to do with foolish arguments. You can just... not go.

When the devil tries to talk, the sermon's answer is the title of this post: I'm going to pretend I didn't hear that. It's not denial. It's declining. There's a difference between ignoring a problem and refusing to negotiate with a liar.

"Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ."2 Corinthians 10:5

03Say it out loud

This was the part that rearranged my thinking: you can speak to God with your mind, but you have to speak to the devil with your mouth.

Silent agreement with the truth isn't enough when the lie is loud. Jesus in the wilderness didn't think His way out of temptation. Three times He answered out loud: "It is written." The spoken word is the weapon; the thought is just the holster.

Romans 10 says confession is made with the mouth. James 4:7 says resist the devil and he will flee, and resistance is not a mood, it's an action. When the devil brings a negative report, you answer with a positive report. Out loud. In Jesus' name, which Philippians says every knee bows to.

"Resist the devil, and he will flee from you."James 4:7

04Take every thought captive

Try it right now. Six of the accuser's greatest hits, one per card. Tap a card to take the thought captive and answer it out loud with the truth.

05The practice

Three steps, in order, for the next time the 2am argument starts:

When the lie shows up

  • Notice the voice. Ask: is this the accuser or the Father? Accusation sounds like shame and "you always." Conviction sounds like love and "come back."
  • Decline the argument. Don't debate it, don't replay it. I'm going to pretend I didn't hear that.
  • Answer out loud. Speak the opposite truth with your mouth, in Jesus' name. The lie was loud, so the truth gets to be loud too.

Changing your language changes your life. That's the whole sermon in one sentence, and honestly it's the whole Christian life in one sentence.
