How Voice AI Hears You

aiengineeringResearched October 2026

Every voice assistant runs the same relay: an ear turns sound into text, a brain turns text into an answer, and a mouth turns the answer back into sound. The ear is the hard part, because sound arrives as a messy pressure wave and meaning hides inside it. This primer walks the whole loop, from waveform to words and back, with the papers that built it and the researchers arguing about where it breaks.

1. The brief

Talk to any voice assistant and you are running a three-stage relay. First, speech recognition turns your voice into text. Whisper, the open model that reset the field in 2022, does it by chopping audio into 30-second chunks, converting each chunk into a picture of its frequencies, and having a Transformer write out the words one token at a time. Second, a language model reads the transcript and thinks, in pure text. Third, a speech synthesizer turns the reply back into a pressure wave your ears accept as a voice. The relay works surprisingly well now: Whisper makes about half the errors of the previous generation on messy real-world audio, and newer systems like Moshi skip the relay entirely, listening and talking at the same time with about 200 milliseconds of delay. But the ear still invents words when it hears silence, and the whole loop still strains on accents, noise, and interruptions. The sentence to remember: voice AI is translation twice over, sound into words and words back into sound, and every failure is a translation failure.

4. Key voices

Four people who built the loop or are stress-testing it. Each view is one or two sentences with the primary source linked. The real action is at the bottom, where the first two collide.

The big disagreement

Same decoder, opposite verdicts. One switch, every tap flips. No picking sides, just the collision.

Alec Radford OpenAI, Whisper team
the prior is the engine

Whisper's decoder is half language model, and that is the point. Trained on 680,000 hours of messy web audio, the model's sense of what words come next cleans up bad acoustics: accents, noise, mumbling. Zero-shot, it makes 50% fewer errors than models fine-tuned on clean benchmarks. Robustness comes from breadth, not from cleaner labels.

Allison Koenecke Cornell
the prior is the failure mode

The same language sense that fixes acoustics invents words when the acoustics go quiet. About 1% of Whisper segments contain hallucinated phrases, 38% of those with explicit harms, and the burden falls hardest on people with aphasia, whose pauses trigger it. A model that writes what it expects to hear is a liability in hospitals and courtrooms. Audit first, deploy second.

Both cite the same architecture. Radford reads the decoder as a denoiser; Koenecke reads it as a confabulator. The field's open question is whether you can keep the cleanup without the invention.

5. What comes next

Every prediction below is attributed: who said it, where, when. The badge is my read of how solid it is: consensus means multiple independent sources agree, contested means smart people disagree, speculative means one analysis's leap. Predictions go stale; this section is frozen as of October 2026.

Full-duplex becomes the default voice interfaceconsensus
Half-duplex turn-taking (speak, wait, listen) gives way to always-listening systems that handle overlap and interruption: Moshi at about 200ms of practical latency (Kyutai, Sep 2024), sub-300ms native systems shipping in 2026, and open-weights turn-taking at about 450ms (NVIDIA VoiceChat-11B, Sep 2026).
Direction stated in "Revolutionizing Voice AI: Enter the Era of Real-Time, Full-Duplex Conversations," Brief, May 6, 2026: "The half-duplex era of voice AI is ending, and the next generation will rely on native, real-time, full-duplex conversation."
"GPT-Live-1 kills the cascaded pipeline"contested
Native speech-to-speech reaching developer APIs ends the STT to LLM to TTS relay, per TechTimes (Sep 12, 2026). The counter: cascaded platforms (ElevenLabs, Vapi, Retell) keep the architecture because every stage is swappable and inspectable, at the cost of irreducible handoff latency and fragility under real-world noise and hesitation. Architecture is now the argument, not accuracy.
Claim and counter both reported in TechTimes, "Full-Duplex Voice AI Hits Developer APIs," Sep 12, 2026.
Voice assistants start hearing how you said itspeculative
End-to-end models keep what transcription throws away: prosody, emotion, speaker identity, background sounds. The leap is responses tuned to tone, not just words. The blocker is measurement: benchmarks still score transcription accuracy, while "conversational feel" has no standard test yet.
Motivation stated in the Moshi paper (Défossez et al., Kyutai, Sep 2024): text as the intermediate modality loses "non-linguistic information that modifies meaning, such as emotion or non-speech sounds." Benchmark gap noted in the Brief full-duplex analysis, May 2026.
Impersonation becomes the binding constraintspeculative
Fluent, emotionally faithful voice synthesis makes speaker identity unverifiable by ear. Expect voice-based authentication to erode before voice interfaces peak, and expect the first serious regulation to target voice cloning rather than transcription.
Concern flagged in the Brief full-duplex analysis, May 2026 ("safety concerns around impersonation and manipulation in fluent, emotionally driven voice systems"); Whisper's own model card warned about surveillance dual-use in 2022.
