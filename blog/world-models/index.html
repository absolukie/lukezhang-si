World Models vs Next-Token Prediction

llmsaiResearched October 2026

There are two stories about how machines get smart. The first says: teach a model to predict the next word at the scale of the whole internet, and understanding falls out. The second says: that is a detour. Language is a thin slice of the world, and a model trained to guess the next token mimics language about the world without learning how the world works. This is the sharpest architecture argument in AI, with a Turing Award winner's $1.03 billion startup on one side and the entire scaling industry on the other. The sentence to remember: both sides agree prediction is how models learn; they disagree about what the prediction should be of.

1. The brief

Yann LeCun, one of deep learning's three Turing laureates, spent years arguing that large language models are the wrong substrate for intelligence. In June 2022 he wrote the position paper that made the case formally: build world models, systems that learn how the world evolves and predict future states in an abstract internal representation, instead of predicting the next token. Then the industry kept scaling tokens, and kept winning benchmarks. Then LeCun left Meta in late 2025, raised $1.03 billion for AMI Labs in March 2026, and started saying the quiet part out loud: LLMs are "a detour" and will be "largely obsolete" within five years.

The other side has receipts of its own. A tiny model trained only to predict Othello moves quietly built an internal 8x8 board, which nobody taught it. That finding, hardened by a 2025 replication across seven model families, is the best evidence that next-token-style training induces world models on its own. So this primer covers both machines: what next-token prediction actually learns, what a world model is, the one architecture move (JEPA) that separates them, the Othello surprise, and where the disagreement is real and where it is theater.

3. The landmarks

Thirteen moments that built the argument, June 2022 to September 2026. No stepper this time, just the record.

Jun 2022
The blueprint

LeCun publishes "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (OpenReview, v0.9.2): configurable predictive world models, behavior driven by intrinsic motivation, and hierarchical JEPA trained with self-supervised learning, predicting in representation space instead of reconstructing inputs.

Oct 2022
The Othello shock

Li et al., "Emergent World Representations" (arXiv:2210.13382): a GPT-style model trained only on Othello move transcripts develops a nonlinear internal representation of the board state, and intervention experiments show the representation controls the model's outputs.

Jan 2023
JEPA's first proof

"Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture" (arXiv:2301.08243): predicting the embeddings of masked image regions learns more semantic representations than pixel reconstruction, at matched compute.

Feb 2024
Video gets a JEPA

V-JEPA (Bardes et al.): the same idea extended across time, masking spatio-temporal regions of video and predicting their embeddings, forcing the model to learn motion and interaction rather than static appearance.

Mar 2025
The Othello result hardens

Yuan and Sogaard "Revisiting the Othello World Model Hypothesis" (arXiv:2503.04421): across seven model families, from GPT-2 to Qwen2.5, every model induces the Othello board layout, up to 99% accuracy in unsupervised grounding.

Sep 2025
"Doomed"

At an NYU Center for Data Science seminar, LeCun declares auto-regressive LLMs "doomed": with per-token error e, the chance of a fully correct answer falls as (1 minus e) to the n, and he argues this exponential compounding is "not fixable (without a major redesign)."

Dec 2025
JEPA learns to talk

VL-JEPA (arXiv:2512.10942): instead of generating tokens one by one, predict continuous embeddings of target text. In a controlled comparison with identical vision encoders and training data, it beats standard token-space VLM training with 50% fewer trainable parameters.

Late 2025
LeCun leaves Meta

After about 12 years as chief AI scientist, he departs to co-found AMI Labs (Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs) in Paris, betting the company on world models built with JEPA.

Jan 2026
"Simply false"

In an MIT Technology Review profile, LeCun says the idea that scaling LLMs reaches human-level intelligence is "simply false." At an MIT symposium he adds that world models will be the dominant AI architecture within three to five years, and "nobody in their right mind" would use today's LLMs.

Mar 2026
The billion-dollar bet

AMI Labs announces a $1.03B seed at a $3.5B pre-money valuation, co-led by Cathay Innovation, Greycroft, Hiro Capital, HV Capital, and Bezos Expeditions, with Nvidia, Toyota, Samsung, and Temasek backing: Europe's largest seed round, funding fundamental world-model research and open-sourcing the results.

May 2026
"A detour"

On Bloomberg's "The Close," LeCun says "Large language models are not the path to real intelligence. They're a detour," and predicts LLMs will be "largely obsolete" within five years. He advises doctoral students not to make LLMs their focus.

Jun 2026
V-JEPA 2

Meta's JEPA scales to over one million hours of video; V-JEPA 2 hits 77.3 top-1 accuracy on Something-Something v2 and state of the art on human action anticipation. V-JEPA 2-AC, post-trained on less than 62 hours of robot video, plans zero-shot pick-and-place on real Franka arms in two labs, with no data from those environments and no task-specific training or reward.

Sep 2026
World models get a big exit

AMD agrees to acquire Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2B, betting that spatial intelligence, models that generate, reconstruct, and simulate interactive 3D environments, is the next step after text.
