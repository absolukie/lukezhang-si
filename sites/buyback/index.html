Questions people ask

Is this legal advice?

No. Buyback is an organizational tool, not a law firm. Lemon law is fact-specific and state-specific. Before you act, talk to a licensed lemon law attorney in your state. Most offer free case reviews and work on contingency.

What counts as the "same problem"?

Use the same short label for the same symptom every visit, for example "transmission shudder." Different dealers count together as long as the complaint is the same. What matters is the recurring defect, not where you took it.

Do days in the shop have to be consecutive?

No. In all fifteen states the day counts are cumulative across visits. Keep every repair order: the date-in and date-out on each invoice is what proves your total.

What if my car is used?

Some states cover used cars still under the manufacturer's original warranty (California does, for example). Texas generally covers new vehicles only. The tool tracks the presumption windows; an attorney can tell you if your used car qualifies another way.

How is this free? How do you make money?

Buyback is free for consumers, and it stays free. If you choose to get matched with a lemon law attorney through the case-review form, participating attorneys may pay us a referral fee. That never changes what you pay: lemon law attorneys work on contingency, so there is no upfront cost to you either way.

Where does my data go?

On your devices: everything you enter is stored in your browser's local storage, and device sync keeps copies on our server so your other devices can read them. Sign-in is optional: it just links your devices for sync, nothing else changes. There is no tracking. Read the full privacy policy.

How do free lemon law case reviews work?

Lemon law attorneys take these cases on contingency: no upfront cost, they are paid from the settlement or buyback. A free review is usually a 10 to 15 minute call where the intake team asks about your car, your repair history, and your state. They are deciding whether your facts fit the firm's criteria, so having dates, dealers, and repair counts ready makes the call short and useful. This tool's print summary was built for exactly that call. No attorney-client relationship forms until you sign an agreement.

The dealer wrote "no problem found." Does the visit still count?

Log it anyway. This app counts every dealer visit as a repair attempt, and attorneys read the whole history. A visit is weakest when the ticket says "no problem found" and your symptom appears nowhere on the page, so make sure the repair order describes the problem in your own words, and keep every invoice, including the ones where they found nothing.

What should I ask on the consult call?

Ask: Do you handle lemon law cases in my state? How many buyback or replacement cases did you close last year? On contingency, what percentage do you take, and who pays the costs if we lose? Should my case aim for repurchase, replacement, or a cash settlement? What is the realistic timeline? What is the next deadline on my calendar? Who will actually work my file day to day? The printed case summary from this tool includes this list with your case facts attached.
