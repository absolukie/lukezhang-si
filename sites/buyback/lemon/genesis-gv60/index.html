ICCU failure draining the 12V battery

The GV60 is covered by the Hyundai/Kia ICCU recall: the charging control unit can become defective and stop charging the 12V battery, pushing the car into failsafe mode. One owner reported the 12V battery dying four times in a few months despite the car supposedly conditioning it automatically. The 2023-2025 GV60 is named in the September 2026 class action over the twice-recalled ICCU.

Owners discussing this: Business Wire on ICCU class action

Recurring dead 12V battery

On the Genesis owners forum, a GV60 owner documented repeated 12V battery deaths starting from delivery, with the dealer unable to explain why the car's automatic conditioning was not keeping the battery alive. Third-party apps like insurance driving trackers can keep the car awake and worsen the drain, but owners report failures even without any connected apps. Dealers have reportedly seen the issue on 2025 models as well.

Owners discussing this: GenesisOwners.com GV60 reported issues thread

Rear driveshaft failure recall

A July 2023 recall covered GV60s whose rear driveshafts could fail, causing a loss of drive. The fix was fitting new replacement rear driveshafts as a countermeasure. This is a rare drivetrain failure for an EV of this age, and owners are advised to confirm recall completion before purchase.

Owners discussing this: WhatCar GV60 used review reliability

Parking brake can release on sensor error

A 2022 recall covered GV60s where a temporary parking brake release was possible when an instantaneous error occurred in the sensing signal. The fix was a software update to correct the signal handling. A parking brake that can release on its own creates a rollaway risk when the car is parked.

Genesis GV60 lemon questions

Is my Genesis GV60 a lemon?

Maybe. It depends on your state's thresholds and your repair history, not on the defect alone. If your GV60 has been in for the same problem 3 or more times, or has spent weeks in the shop, log every visit in the tracker and let it do the counting against your state's statute.

What should I do after each dealer visit?

Get the repair order before you leave, check that the complaint is written the way you described it, and note the date in, date out, and mileage. Photograph the invoice and log the visit the same day. Gaps in paperwork are how winnable cases get lost.

Do the repair attempts have to be for the same problem?

For the repeat-repair routes, generally yes: the same defect, attempted and failed multiple times. The days-out-of-service route is different, it is cumulative across all warranty defects. The tracker counts both separately so you can see every path.

Should I talk to a lawyer now or keep waiting?

Lemon law attorneys offer free case reviews and work on contingency, so an early conversation costs nothing. But whatever you decide, keep logging: evidence you collect now is what makes a case review turn into a case.
