Rear-view camera failure persisting after two recalls

NHTSA opened a recall query into 27,816 Polestar 2 vehicles (2021-2025) after 109 complaints that the rear-view camera shows 'camera temporarily unavailable' instead of a live image when reversing, raising crash risk. Polestar issued two recalls with software fixes, but the company confirmed the remedy did not correct the original issue. Complaints kept coming even after both patches.

Digital key failures, sensor faults, software speed limits

Owners document digital key failures that require dealer visits, random sensor malfunctions, and software-imposed speed limits, all recurring across the model line. Polestar's own software chief departed as quality complaints piled up. Owners on Reddit report the same faults reappearing after over-the-air updates, making them chronic rather than one-off.

TCAM connectivity failures, emergency calling lost

Telematics and connectivity module (TCAM) failures are a documented owner complaint, knocking out emergency calling, GPS location, and remote app features. They usually do not stop the car from driving, but owners lose safety-critical connectivity and must visit a dealer for a fix. Repeated TCAM failures of safety-related systems can strengthen warranty and lemon-law claims in some states.

12-volt system faults and no-start scenarios

Some Polestar 2 builds suffer unexpected 12-volt system behavior that produces no-start scenarios, addressed by reprogramming the DC-DC charging logic and replacing the 12V battery where needed. A dead 12V battery in an EV leaves the car completely inert, even with a full main battery. Region-specific campaigns cover this on select early builds.

Polestar 2 lemon questions

Is my Polestar 2 a lemon?

Maybe. It depends on your state's thresholds and your repair history, not on the defect alone. If your 2 has been in for the same problem 3 or more times, or has spent weeks in the shop, log every visit in the tracker and let it do the counting against your state's statute.

What should I do after each dealer visit?

Get the repair order before you leave, check that the complaint is written the way you described it, and note the date in, date out, and mileage. Photograph the invoice and log the visit the same day. Gaps in paperwork are how winnable cases get lost.

Do the repair attempts have to be for the same problem?

For the repeat-repair routes, generally yes: the same defect, attempted and failed multiple times. The days-out-of-service route is different, it is cumulative across all warranty defects. The tracker counts both separately so you can see every path.

Should I talk to a lawyer now or keep waiting?

Lemon law attorneys offer free case reviews and work on contingency, so an early conversation costs nothing. But whatever you decide, keep logging: evidence you collect now is what makes a case review turn into a case.
