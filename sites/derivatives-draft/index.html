Derivatives, the mastery sheet

Everything about derivatives on one page: the one idea, four derivations from scratch, the secant that becomes a tangent, the power-rule shortcut, where derivatives break, and flashcards to prove you know it.

1. The one idea

A derivative is the slope of a curve at a single point. Not between two points, that is just an average. At one point, exactly, right now. Speedometers, hill steepness under your feet, how fast a rumor is spreading this minute: all derivatives.

The definition

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$

Read it as: take the slope between two points $h$ apart, then crush $h$ to zero. Whatever the slope settles on is the derivative. The whole sheet is this one sentence, unpacked.

The algebra behind the readout: $\frac{(2+h)^2 - 4}{h} = \frac{4h + h^2}{h} = 4 + h$. Every term except the 4 carries an $h$, so as $h \to 0$ the slope has nowhere to go but 4. That limit is the derivative: $f'(2) = 4$.

4. The shortcut: the power rule

After the third derivation you stop doing limits by hand. The pattern is always the same, so it gets a name:

Bring down the power, subtract one

$$\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1}$$

Why it works in one line: $(x+h)^n$ expands to $x^n + n \cdot x^{n-1} \cdot h +$ terms with $h^2$ or more. The $x^n$ cancels, dividing by $h$ leaves $n \cdot x^{n-1}$ plus terms still holding an $h$, and those vanish as $h \to 0$. Sections 2 and 3 were this sentence, four times, with numbers.

d/dx of x to the 7th7x to the 6th
d/dx of x to the 100th100x to the 99th
d/dx of 1/xminus 1 over x squared

That last one: $1/x = x^{-1}$, so the rule gives $-1 \cdot x^{-2}$. The rule does not care that the power is negative.

5. When to use it

Three jobs. Speedometers, hilltops, and the entire Taylor sheet run on this one idea.

Motion: velocity is the slope of position

If your position over time is $p(t) = t^2$ meters, your speedometer reads the derivative: $v(t) = 2t$. Drag time and watch the tangent's slope match your speed.

Optimization: the flat tangent finds the peak

Throw a ball: height $h(x) = -(x-2)^2 + 4$. Where is it highest? Exactly where the tangent goes flat, slope zero. Drag x and find it.

The move has a name: set the derivative to zero and solve. $h'(x) = -2(x-2) = 0$ gives $x = 2$. Every "find the best" problem in ML, economics, and engineering is this move wearing a costume.
