A new way to answer

A new generation of our models is now reaching everyone. It keeps the strong reasoning people already rely on, and it adds a whole new surface for the answers themselves.

The model can now respond with visuals and interactive elements that are chosen to fit the question. Comparisons can sit side by side instead of stacking in a wall of text. Explanations can become diagrams you explore with a finger.

Plain text is still there when it is clearest. The point is that the answer now takes the shape that fits the idea, and the model decides that shape on the fly.

Everyday questions, answered visually

Some questions are easier to answer with pictures, plans, and lists than with plain paragraphs. These snapshots show how a reply can turn into a menu, a wardrobe, or a weekend on the road.

5.6 Instant

A Sunday lamb roast for friends is a classic menu: roast lamb, crispy potatoes, a green salad, peas with mint, and apple crumble to finish. It looks generous on the table and most of the work happens in the oven.

Go for a bone-in leg of lamb. Season it with salt, garlic, and rosemary the night before so the flavor sinks in. Roast it low and slow first, then finish with a hot blast for a crisp, golden crust. Let it rest before carving.

Run the day in this order. Get the lamb into the oven first. Parboil the potatoes and rough up the edges while it roasts, then add them to the oven for the last stretch. Assemble the salad and warm the peas near the end. Slide the crumble in to warm while the lamb rests, and make gravy from the pan drippings.

Buy a little more than you think you need. Leftovers make excellent Monday sandwiches, and running out in front of guests is worse than having too much.

    How it works

    Two pieces make this possible. First, a library of interface components that the model can assemble: sliders, maps, diagrams, comparison panes, and more. Second, a compiler that streams the interface into the page while the answer is still being generated, so visuals appear as you read.

    We also trained the model on layout and interaction judgment. It learns when a visual helps, when plain text wins, and how much complexity a reader can absorb at a glance.

    Component library A reusable kit of visual parts the model reaches for as it answers. Streaming compiler Interfaces render live, piece by piece, while the answer generates. Design judgment Training that teaches the model when visuals help and when text wins.

    Frontier intelligence, delivered faster

    The newest model no longer waits until its thinking is finished to start talking. It reasons and answers in the same pass, so useful partial answers reach you while the deeper reasoning continues in the background.

    That interleaved design matters most on hard questions. Instead of staring at a thinking indicator for the full wait, you get a real answer forming earlier, and the complete reasoning still arrives by the end.

    Deploying safely

    Visual answers carry new kinds of risk. A chart can mislead faster than a paragraph, and an interactive demo can feel convincing before it is checked. We built risk awareness into the training so the model weighs these tradeoffs when it chooses a format.

    The model is also taught to be honest about its limits, to decline requests that could cause harm, and to hold up under adversarial testing. An outside team of testers tried to break the safeguards before launch, and their findings shaped what we shipped.

    Read the system card Full details on testing, risks, and mitigations.

    Availability

    Intelligent UI is rolling out now. Paid tiers get it first, and free tiers will follow in the coming weeks as we expand capacity and finish early evaluations.

    For workplace plans, availability depends on your admin settings. If your team has kept new features on, you will see the new answer types appear automatically.

    What comes next

    For decades, people have bent around software. Menus, forms, and fixed layouts decide how we ask and how we answer. Intelligent UI is a step toward software that bends around people instead.

    This is early. The formats will get richer, the judgment will get sharper, and the interface will keep disappearing into the task itself. We are excited to see what you ask it first.
