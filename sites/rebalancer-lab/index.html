What am I looking at?

Every task has a cost for running on every machine (the matrix below). The solver's job: give each task exactly one machine, each machine gets a fair share, and the total cost is as low as possible. Trying every combination is impossible (24 tasks on 6 machines has more options than atoms in the universe, roughly). So Rebalancer uses local search: start random, then repeatedly try swapping two tasks. Keep the swap if the total drops. Stop when nothing helps. It's not guaranteed perfect, but at Meta's scale it's fast and close.

The real numbers

From Meta's announcement: Rebalancer has run in production for 9+ years, solves ~40 million assignment problems per day across 30+ formulations (hardware placement, service placement, traffic routing), and hits P99 of 12 seconds on a problem with 265,000 objects and 3,200 bins. It ships two solvers: an optimal MIP solver for when you need the true best answer, and the parallelized local search you just watched for when you need a great answer fast. C++ and Python, at github.com/facebook/rebalancer.
