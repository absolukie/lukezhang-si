Title

Paths to redemption after a permanent ban

¶2 The verdict

Verdict box

CURRENT

A permanent ban with no way back doesn't reform anyone. It just exiles them. Ludwig's argument, from a Minecraft speedrunning ban: replace permanent conduct bans with long timed bans and an explicit appeal path. Punishment without a route to redemption makes the community worse, not better.

¶3 The setup: who and what

What happened

CURRENT

EDCR, one of the best Minecraft speedrunners in the world and last year's MCSR season 11 playoff winner, was permanently banned from future playoffs by the Minecraft ranked team. Old Discord DMs from 2023–24 surfaced mid-playoffs; he was 16–17 when he sent them, 19 now. The messages were homophobic, transphobic, and racist. About as bad as it gets.

¶4 Disqualified, denied, apologized

What happened

CURRENT

He was disqualified from the playoffs immediately, denied sending the messages the same day, then posted a public apology two days later. After the playoffs, the ranked team finalized it: a permanent ban. Ludwig agrees the disqualification was the right call. It's the permanence he disputes.

¶5 Ludwig's quote: the core distinction

Permanent vs. indefinite

CURRENT

“When you do a permanent ban as opposed to an indefinite ban, it removes the ability for somebody to grow.”

¶6 The proposal

Permanent vs. indefinite

CURRENT

That's the core distinction. His proposal: a one-to-two-year ban, then the right to appeal. Not an automatic unban, an appeal. Reviewers look at how he's changed, anecdotes from friends, his conduct in the community. Not enough change? Another year, appeal again. In five years he'll be a very different person; a permanent ban means he has no recourse no matter who he becomes.

¶7 Ludwig's quote: the path

¶14 No star treatment

Skill and cheating

CURRENT

Three of the top ~10 players are reportedly banned. Being good at the game earns no leniency, and the same appeal policy should apply to all of them. Cheating bans stay in their own category: those exist as deterrence, a warning to everyone else. Conduct is where rehabilitation can actually work.

¶15 The Tyler1 proof

Skill and cheating

CURRENT

The proof it can work is Tyler1: “wildly toxic,” banned, then “this beautiful redemption arc where he became the face of the game.” With a caveat Ludwig states plainly: it's not one-to-one. EDCR's messages were “much worse than anything Tyler said in game,” so the ban stays long. But the arc is possible.

¶16 Ludwig's quote: grace online vs off

The empathy gap

CURRENT

“The internet never wants to give redemption... in person [people] are super down to give redemption. Online there's so much less grace and empathy given.”

¶17 Two standards

The empathy gap

CURRENT

His observation: the same people who'd forgive face-to-face default to “rot in hell” behind a screen. He used to think the answer was “leave and build connections in a new community.” For an ordinary player, that's fine. But for someone whose identity is the game, there's nowhere equivalent to go. The appeal test stays practical, not theatrical: judge by how he operates in the community now and what the people around him say.

¶18 The intro line

Parallels

CURRENT

The argument isn't new. It keeps showing up wherever punishment exists.

¶19 The prodigal son

Parallels

CURRENT

The prodigal son (Luke 15). The son isn't auto-restored when the money runs out. He has to get up and walk home. The father was watching the road, but the son initiated. That's the appeal mechanism exactly: the path exists, but the punished has to choose to walk it. And the father doesn't pretend nothing happened. The feast is because he came back, not as if he never left.

¶20 David

Parallels

CURRENT

David (2 Samuel 12). Confronted by Nathan, David confesses immediately: “I have sinned against the Lord.” Contrast EDCR, who denied it the same day. David still faced real consequences, but he wasn't discarded as king. Honesty kept the door open; lying is what nearly slams it. Same pricing as Ludwig's.

¶21 The TRC

Parallels

CURRENT

South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission. Amnesty wasn't handed out; it was traded for full, honest disclosure. Tell the whole truth, get a path back; lie or hide, get none. Confession opens the appeal, lying extends the ban. The TRC ran Ludwig's policy at national scale.

¶22 Versailles vs. the Marshall Plan

Parallels

CURRENT

Versailles vs. the Marshall Plan. After WWI, the Treaty of Versailles punished Germany punitively: humiliation, crushing reparations, no path back to dignity. It bred resentment and a worse war. After WWII, the Marshall Plan rebuilt former enemies into allies. Punitive peace vs. restorative peace, tested at the largest scale available. Sun Tzu's escape route, with empires.

¶23 Why it stuck with me

Closer

CURRENT

The idea is older than Minecraft: no one is beyond restoration while they're still willing to change. Grace with a process: not cheap, not absent. A ban that says “show us who you've become in two years” does more work than one that says “never.”
