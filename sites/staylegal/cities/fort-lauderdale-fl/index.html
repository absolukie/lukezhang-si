Is Airbnb legal in Fort Lauderdale, Florida?

Legal with a $880 city registration, no night cap. Fort Lauderdale allows whole-home investor rentals through its Vacation Rental Registration Program. A new registration costs $880 including the first safety inspection, and renewal is $650 a year for non-owner-occupied properties. Occupancy is capped at two guests per legal bedroom, and condo HOAs are the most common dealbreaker.

Registration checklist

  1. 1
    Line up state and county first
    The city requires the Florida DBPR license, a Broward County business tax receipt, and county tourist tax registration before it accepts your application.
  2. 2
    Apply for the city certificate
    File the Vacation Rental Registration application, pay $880, and pass the city safety inspection.
  3. 3
    Keep it current
    Renew annually by the August 1 deadline to avoid being treated as a new applicant and paying the full $880 again.

What could change this verdict

Your HOA
Most condo buildings ban rentals under 30 days in their governing documents, which Florida law lets them enforce.
Fines are steep
The city can fine $1,000 per day for operating without a valid certificate.

Hosted stays need the same certificate

The registration program covers any whole or partial unit rented more than three times a year. A resident renting rooms follows the same registration and inspection process.
