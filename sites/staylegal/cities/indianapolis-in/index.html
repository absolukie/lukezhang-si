Is Airbnb legal in Indianapolis, Indiana?

Legal with a city permit; state law bars local bans. Since January 1, 2025, every STR in Indianapolis needs its own annual permit from the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services under Chapter 852 of the Revised Code. The initial permit is $150 per unit with free renewals, and Indiana state law protects STRs from outright local bans.

Key limits

Per-unit permits
Each STR unit is permitted individually; operating without one is a Class C infraction with every booking counted separately.
Safety standards
Working smoke and CO detectors, fire extinguishers, and posted emergency contact information are required.
Local contact
A 24/7 local contact must be designated for emergencies.
Occupancy
Guest limits are tied to property size, typically around two guests per bedroom plus two additional.

Taxes

State sales tax
7% Indiana gross retail tax on every booking
County innkeeper's tax
Up to 10% in Marion County; Airbnb and Vrbo collect and remit both on platform bookings

What could change this verdict

Your HOA
HOA and condo covenants can still ban STRs regardless of state protection, so read the governing documents.
Rule changes
A 2026 Indiana law bars cities from capping rental use of residential property, with older local caps getting until January 1, 2028 to comply.

Legal with permit

Indiana law protects owner-occupied STRs as a residential use

Since 2018, STR of an owner's primary residence has been a permitted residential use in any district that allows housing, and no zoning ordinance may disallow it.
