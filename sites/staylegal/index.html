    Key terms, plainly

    The five phrases that show up in every verdict, explained the way a host would explain them.

    Transient occupancy tax

    A nightly tax on short stays, like the hotel tax on a hotel room. It is usually a percentage of what the guest pays, often 10 to 15 percent.

    Airbnb and Vrbo collect and send this tax for you in many cities, but not all. Your verdict shows who handles it.

    Business license

    The city's general permission slip to run a business. It is separate from the short-term rental permit.

    Some cities make you get the business license first, then apply for the rental permit. Skipping it is one of the most common fines.

    Night cap

    The maximum number of nights per year you may rent the place short-term. Los Angeles caps you at 120, for example.

    Go over the cap and you are operating without permission, even if you hold a valid permit. The city can audit your calendar.

    Primary residence rule

    You must actually live in the home most of the year to qualify. Los Angeles requires 6 plus months; San Francisco requires 275 plus nights.

    This is the rule that blocks most investors. A dedicated rental property you never live in fails this test in strict cities.

    HOA and CC&Rs

    Your building's own rulebook. CC&Rs are the Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions recorded against the property.

    An HOA or condo board can ban short-term rentals even where the city allows them. City permission never overrides your building's ban. Run the HOA red-flag check below.

    Questions people ask

    Is this legal advice?

    No. StayLegal is a research starting point built from city ordinances and official guides. Rules change often and vary by zoning, HOA, and building type. Always confirm with the city's planning or business-license office before you buy.

    How current is the data?

    Every city page shows when its rules were last researched (currently October 2026). Cities update STR ordinances frequently, so treat the report as a head start, not the final word.

    What if my city is not covered yet?

    The interactive checker covers 34 cities so far. We also publish researched short-term rental rule guides for 103 cities and counting: browse all city guides. If yours is missing from both, pick the nearest covered city only as a rough sense of how strict the region is, not as an answer for your address.

    Does a "legal" verdict mean my HOA allows it?

    No. An HOA, condo board, or lease can ban short-term rentals even where the city allows them. The report flags this, but you have to check your own documents.

    Why does the verdict change when I toggle "I live there"?

    Many cities treat hosted stays (you present during the visit) far more leniently than investor-owned whole-home rentals. New York is the starkest example: whole-home short stays are banned, hosted stays are registrable.
