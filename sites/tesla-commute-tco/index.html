How charging actually works: the real routine

Your work charger adds ~25% in 2 hours, which makes it Level 2 (~7.5 kW), not DC fast. That changes everything: one session covers about a third of an office day, not the whole thing. Here is the routine that closes the gap.

1

Know your real numbers

One office day burns ~42 kWh (127-mile round trip, ~47 from Brentwood). One 2-hour work session adds ~15 kWh (25% of the 60 kWh LFP pack). Weekly: you burn ~116 kWh, work gives back ~30 kWh. The ~86 kWh gap comes from Superchargers, unless home charging enters the picture. Play with the toggles above to see each scenario priced live.

2

The Sunday fill: Brentwood, Streets of Brentwood

2455 Sand Creek Rd, 16 stalls up to 250 kW, open 24/7. Arrive around 15-20%, charge toward 90% (~35-40 min), and do your grocery run while it fills. This is the backbone of the week. Off-peak rates (~$0.29/kWh, typically 9pm-11am) beat peak (~$0.50+) so a weekend morning is kind to the wallet too. Always check the exact rate on the car's nav pin before plugging in.

3

The Thursday top-up: I-680 corridor, on the way home

Concord, 1975 Diamond Blvd (19 fast chargers) or Walnut Creek, Ygnacio Plaza, 1881 Ygnacio Valley Rd (24 fast chargers), both right off your route home. 20-30 min while you grab dinner. Skip Pleasanton Johnson Dr (72 kW, slow) unless desperate. Office-day alternative: top up at lunch at the East Palo Alto Supercharger, 1771 E Bayshore Rd, minutes from the office, or Menlo Park, 325 Sharon Park Dr. No detour on the drive home. Golden rule: arrive low, leave by 80%. The 10-80% zone is the fast part of the curve; past 80% crawls.

4

Second work session = bonus, not plan

When you snag a second 2-hour session, that is 15 kWh you do not Supercharge, roughly $7 saved at peak rates. Do not build the routine around it, but take it when it is free. Flip the "work sessions" toggle above to see the upside.

5

The rental-house hack: a plain wall outlet

Renting does not rule out home charging. A regular 120V garage outlet + Tesla mobile connector (~$200) adds ~14 kWh overnight: five nights = 70 kWh a week at home electric rates (~$0.30/kWh), nearly erasing the Supercharger bill. A dryer outlet (NEMA 14-30 + ~$35 adapter) is full Level 2 with zero electrician. When house hunting, check for a garage outlet and ask the landlord; most say yes. Toggle it above to see the math.

The key point: work charging alone does not sustain 2 days a week. The routine is one weekend fill plus one midweek top-up, ~$25-45/week in Supercharging depending on peak vs off-peak. A garage wall outlet is the single highest-leverage upgrade.

🛣️ The commute, realistically

Antioch → Menlo Park is not a short drive, and Brentwood tacks on ~13 more miles round trip. Here is the honest picture at 2 days a week. Toggle your home base in the controls above.

Route

CA-4 W → CA-242 S → I-680 S → CA-84 W over the Dumbarton Bridge → US-101 S into Menlo Park. About 63–68 miles each way from Antioch, ~70–75 from Brentwood.

Time

80–110 minutes each way in rush hour. Leave Antioch ~7:00am → desk ~8:30. Leave ~5:30pm → home ~7:00–7:15. That's ~3 hours in the car per office day, ~6 hours a week.

Toll

Dumbarton is $8.50 westbound with FasTrak (2026 rate, rising yearly to $10.50 by 2030). You pay it every morning; the evening return is free. ≈ $880/year at 2 days/week.

No carpool lane

California's Clean Air Vehicle decal program ended Sept 30, 2025. A 2022 Model 3 gets no HOV access. You're in the regular lanes on 680 with everyone else. This is exactly where FSD earns its $99.

Where FSD shines

The grind is stop-and-go highway, FSD's best use case: CA-4 through Pittsburg/Concord, 680 through San Ramon, the Dumbarton approach backup. You'll take over for the 84/101 interchange dance and Menlo Park surface streets.

Energy per day

~3.0 mi/kWh ≈ 42 kWh per office day from Antioch, ~47 from Brentwood: about 70-80% of the LFP pack. You arrive home with ~30% left. Totally comfortable, no range anxiety, ever.

🤖 FSD on a 2022: the honest picture

A 2022 Model 3 runs Hardware 3 (HW3). Here's what that means for Full Self-Driving in late 2026: the good, the capped, and the dead end.

What you get

FSD v14 Lite (rolling out to HW3 since July 2026), a distilled build of the v14 stack: supervised city + highway driving: lane changes, merges, stop-and-go traffic, traffic lights, unprotected turns. It handles your exact commute (CA-4/680 stop-and-go grind) well. ~120 miles between interventions in real-world fleet data.

The hardware ceiling

HW3 has 1/8 the memory bandwidth of HW4. FSD v13's neural nets (7.5 GB) physically cannot fit on HW3's chips. Tesla had to ship a pruned, quantized v12 instead. Newer cars on HW4/AI4 get ~450 miles between interventions.

No unsupervised future

Elon confirmed on the Q1 2026 earnings call: HW3 will never achieve unsupervised FSD. The v14 Lite build now rolling out stays supervised: hands on wheel, eyes on road, forever. If robotaxi-style autonomy matters to you, a 2022 is a dead end.

The verdict for your commute

For 2 days/week of highway stop-and-go, FSD on HW3 is genuinely good: that's FSD's strongest scenario. Subscribe at $99/mo, never buy outright ($8k+ for a depreciating, capped platform makes no sense). If you go 5 days/week or want the latest, a new HW4 car pulls ahead.

Bottom line: 2022 FSD is a very good supervised highway assistant with no upgrade path. Price it as a $99/mo convenience, not an appreciating asset.
